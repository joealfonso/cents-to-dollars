Pitch accuracy

01

Play a steady test tone of a known pitch near your laptop or phone. The app listens for 5 seconds and tells you how far its reading is from the real pitch.

You need: a tone generator app or a tuning fork app, playing near your laptop or phone.

Not run yet.
How it works: pitch accuracy check

The app takes the median of every pitch reading over 5 seconds and compares it with the frequency you entered, in cents. "Steadiness" is the 95th percentile of how far single readings sit from that median. It needs at least 50 readings.

Screen delay

02

Plays 10 short beeps through your speakers and times how long each one takes to show up on screen.

You need: speakers on (not headphones) and the laptop or phone able to hear them. Turn the volume down first.

Not run yet.
How it works: screen delay check

Each beep is a 440 Hz tone, 0.4 seconds long. The time runs from when the sound leaves the audio output to when the matching pitch is drawn on screen, so it includes the speaker, the room, your laptop's or phone's sound input and the analysis, up to the moment the pitch is drawn.

Built-in accuracy test

03

Runs test recordings with known pitches through the same analyser and reports how close it got. It doesn't listen.

You need: the dev server running, with the test files in place.

Not run yet.
How it works: built-in accuracy test

The synthetic fixtures (steady sine tones at 48 and 44.1 kHz, and sine tones with vibrato) are rendered through the real AudioWorklet in an OfflineAudioContext and scored against the known pitch. "Octave mistakes" counts readings more than 600 cents off.
